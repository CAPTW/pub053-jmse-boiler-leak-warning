Supplementary Methods, Results and Reproducibility
For: Feedwater-Demand Residuals for Early Tube-Leak Warning in a Closed-Loop Auxiliary-Boiler Simulation
Author-review supplement R2, 16 September 2026. This document accompanies a stored-data manuscript analysis; it 
is not a report of new boiler simulations.
S1. Evidence scope and analysis status
The primary research input is the owner-supplied archive 
observable_baseline_study_20260915T215200Z_be142b32.zip. It contains a source copy, applied 
configurations, recorded trajectories, detector traces and the original methods/results working section. The present 
manuscript preparation performed stored-data analysis only. It did not rerun the simulator, generate new operating 
blocks, train a new prognostic model, submit a paper or change repository visibility.
Three evidence layers are kept distinct: (i) original B0/B1 study records; (ii) reconstruction of those records from 
observation projections and stored score traces; and (iii) the newly added post-hoc comparison of separately 
thresholded flow-only and demand-only scores. The third layer reuses an already inspected evaluation set and must 
not be described as prospective confirmation. Earlier conformal-RUL experiments are not evidence for the claims in 
this paper.
The delivered NPZ files are non-pickled, column-preserving projections. They contain all 10,801 timestamps for each 
of the 24 main episodes (259,224 rows), not only selected successful intervals. episode_metadata.csv binds each 
projection to hashes of the original episode, both detector traces, applied configuration, event record and run metadata. 
Absolute Windows locations are retained only in explicitly identified original metadata; the derived block-design tape 
fields are portable basenames.
A bounded flat-primitive Parquet reader was used to export the projections because a general-purpose Parquet engine 
was unavailable in the preparation environment. Its supported encodings and limitations are stated in the code. Earlier 
independent review compared this reader with separately retained CSV series. In this preparation, raw observation 
projections were used to reconstruct scores and fitting, while an independent interval alarm implementation checked 
the sequential alarm state machine. Neither check constitutes independent physical validation.

S2.2. Furnace, pressure-dependent lines and the leak
In the tube-leak study the furnace is not directly degraded. The constructor sizes maximum fuel delivery and heat-
transfer conductance from nominal steam duty and the combustion-efficiency draw. Fuel command controls fuel rate; 
the configured fuel is MGO, excess-air ratio 1.15, wall-loss fraction 0.015 and inlet-air temperature 25 °C. The 
furnace balances gas enthalpy loss against a gas-to-saturation-temperature logarithmic-mean-temperature-difference 
heat-transfer relation. Net drum heat is the nonnegative water-side heat transfer after the configured wall loss. There is 
no superheater in this selected backend. This is a description of the implemented reduced furnace, not a new 
combustion validation.

S3. Exact score construction and fitted constants
Let p and l denote currently available conditioned observations, and u the applied feedwater command. Only causal 
windows are used. Invalid input resets alarm persistence; it is not filled using a future sample or plant truth.
For B0, a = 1 − exp(−2/120). The EWMA starts from the first available deviation, not from a fabricated zero residual, 
and updates as e(t) = e(t−2) + a[d(t)−e(t−2)]. Its score is max(|e_l|/σ_l, |e_p|/σ_p), where d_l=l−0.85 m and d_p=p−7 
bar.
For flow/control features, the 600 s window includes 301 timestamps. Flow integration is trapezoidal; trailing means 
are arithmetic means of those samples. For h = clip(l,0,1.5), the horizontal-cylinder volume is
V(h) = 5[0.75² arccos((0.75−h)/0.75) − (0.75−h)√max(1.5h−h²,0)].
M̂=ρ_l(p)V(h). The density implementation reproduces the original saturated-density interpolation convention: a 
1,000-knot geometric pressure grid over 0.5–120 bar, cubic interpolation, and detector input clipping to 0.5–30 bar. 
The independently evaluated IF97 formulation supplies the grid values. This is an implementation check, not external 
fluid-property or plant validation.
The drum-only imbalance r M is Equation (3) of the manuscript. With q̄_s the trailing mean steam flow, l̄_d the 
trailing mean level deviation and ū_fw the trailing mean command:
r_ref = a₀ + a₁q̄_s + a₂l̄_d; u_ref = b₀ + b₁q̄_s.
Both models are ordinary least-squares fits on the pooled development-normal monitoring rows. Population residual 
standard deviations (ddof=0) are used. The finite availability and window policies are unchanged in the component 
comparison. Regression is an empirical adjustment of normal imbalance, not an identification of individual 
unobserved physical contributions.

S5. Event, censoring and model-domain accounting
The functional predicate is applied feedwater command ≥0.95 AND oracle drum level ≤0.75 m. It must remain true 
for 120 s of elapsed time. On a 2 s grid this spans 61 inclusive timestamps. The start of the retrospectively confirmed 
interval and its online confirmation are both retained. The primary endpoint is confirmation; strict early warning is 
alarm confirmation before the interval starts.
Normal records have no fault endpoint. Seven evaluation fault episodes have an endpoint, one is horizon-censored, 
and no process trip precedes the primary functional confirmation. A censored episode is not assigned a zero lead or a 
pseudo-failure at the horizon.
